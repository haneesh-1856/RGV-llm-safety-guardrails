You: Conversational pacing / turn-taking research (from linguistics and UX for chatbots): give more on this

Agent [Small talk]: Here's more detail on that area:

**From linguistics (conversation analysis):**
- Human conversations naturally follow turn-taking rules where each turn is roughly balanced — one party doesn't just fire question after question without the other getting to contribute something beyond a bare answer. When this balance breaks (one side purely interrogating), it starts to feel like an interview or interrogation rather than a conversation. Linguists call this an asymmetric turn structure, and it's been linked to feelings of being "grilled."
- **Adjacency pairs**: conversation naturally happens in pairs (question→answer, greeting→greeting). But healthy dialogue also includes a third move — an acknowledgment or follow-up comment — before the next question starts. Chatbots that skip this third move (go straight question→answer→next question) feel more mechanical.

**From chatbot/UX research specifically:**
- Studies on conversational AI (customer service bots, health bots, onboarding flows) consistently find a **drop-off cliff around the 3rd-5th consecutive question** if there's no acknowledgment, paraphrase, or "mini-conclusion" inserted.
- **Progressive disclosure**: interfaces (and conversation flows) that reveal one thing at a time, and briefly confirm understanding before the next ask, sustain engagement longer than flows that ask several things in a row.
- **Perceived effort vs. actual effort**: research on form abandonment (this maps onto conversational abandonment too) shows that people don't quit because a form/question sequence is objectively long — they quit when it doesn't visibly progress. Showing "you're 2 of 5 steps in" or reflecting progress verbally ("okay, that narrows it down a lot") reduces abandonment even when total question count is unchanged.
- **Rapport-building bots research** (e.g., studies on mental health chatbots like Woebot) shows that bots which insert brief empathetic reflections between questions ("that sounds frustrating") have higher session completion rates than bots that ask diagnostic-style questions back-to-back.

The throughline: pacing isn't about reducing question count — it's about inserting non-question turns (acknowledgment, reflection, mini-progress marker) between the questions so the exchange still feels like a back-and-forth rather than a checklist.
