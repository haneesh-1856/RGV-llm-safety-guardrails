Agent [Small talk]: Grief is the emotional response to losing something significant — it's less a single feeling and more a mix that can shift day to day, even hour to hour.

**How it can feel:**
- A heavy, hollow, or empty feeling in the chest
- Waves of sadness that come and go, sometimes triggered by small things (a smell, a song, a memory)
- Numbness or feeling disconnected, like things aren't quite real
- Anger or irritability that seems to come out of nowhere
- Guilt — "could I have done something differently"
- Fatigue, trouble sleeping or concentrating
- Occasional relief or normal moments, which can then bring guilt for feeling okay

**How it can "sound" — in what someone says or how they act:**
- Withdrawing from people or activities they used to enjoy
- Talking about the person/thing they lost a lot, or avoiding the topic entirely
- Crying easily, or seeming flat and unreactive
- Changes in appetite, sleep, or energy
- Trouble focusing at work or in conversations
- Sometimes irritability or snapping over small things

**Signs someone might be grieving**, even if they haven't said so directly: they seem "off" from their usual self, they've pulled back from things they normally engage with, their emotions seem closer to the surface than usual, or they mention feeling tired, foggy, or "not themselves" a lot.
